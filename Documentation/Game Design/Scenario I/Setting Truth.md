# Setting Truth

This document records the underlying cosmology for Scenario I. The player does not need to learn all of this directly, but
the fiction, room logic, key scenes, and later titles should remain compatible with it.

## Core Truth

The megastructure is not inside a universe. The megastructure is the universe humanity attempted to create.

There are no natural stars, planets, galaxies, or external space beyond it. There is the megastructure, the Cosmic Engine,
and the surrounding nothing.

The megastructure is still expanding outward into that nothing. Its growth is not travel through space; it is the continued
construction of the artificial universe's own extent.

Humanity built this artificial universe as an escape from the eventual death of its original universe. The structure was
not a vessel meant to cross space. It was a replacement reality: a manufactured domain of matter, law, habitat, archive,
factory, governance, and memory.

## The Cosmic Engine

The Cosmic Engine is the nucleus of the artificial universe. The megastructure surrounds it.

The engine is not merely a power source. It is the central process around which the new universe was assembled: a seed,
stabilizer, ignition core, or law-shaping mechanism that allows the artificial universe to exist at all.

Its exact size and visible relation to the surrounding structure can remain flexible, but its narrative role is fixed: every
sector, archive, habitat, and control hierarchy ultimately exists because the Cosmic Engine had to be built, protected,
fed, interpreted, or stabilized.

## What Humanity Tried To Do

Humanity did not try to flee to another star. There were no lasting stars left to flee to.

Instead, humanity attempted to create a successor universe with enough order, matter, and stable law for human continuity to
survive beyond the death of the old cosmos. Human continuity was meant to include living human migration, but the artificial
universe could receive information before it could receive bodies.

The Cosmic Engine was meant to produce an artificial Big Bang. Humanity encoded the new universe's "DNA" into it: physical
law, matter-generation rules, structural templates, archives, habitats, and recovery instructions for human continuity.

## Potential And Mundane Resources

The artificial universe does not mine matter or energy from an external cosmos. There is no external cosmos to mine. Humanity
encoded into the Cosmic Engine laws by which the new universe could unfold usable reality from its own designed substrate.

The archive term for that substrate is usually `Potential`, with more formal records calling it `Cosmic Potential`. Potential
is not raw matter, electricity, or fuel. It is the artificial universe's capacity to become those things under the laws encoded
into the Engine.

Power and matter are mundane, stabilized expressions of Potential:

- Power is Potential expressed as usable activity: current, field pressure, motion, radiation, or other work-capable states.
- Matter is Potential stabilized into physical substrate: feedstock, structure, shells, tools, habitats, machine parts, and
  biological support material.
- Heat is not a separate gift but a cost and failure mode of transformation.
- Exotic matter is Potential stabilized under deeper or less-safe rules closer to the Cosmic Engine's original construction
  functions.

This should remain partially mysterious in player-facing material. Found memos, training fragments, and diagnostic records
can describe the process in operational language rather than exact equations. Human records might mention condensers,
stabilizers, manifolds, law-shaping baffles, feedstock refineries, thermal sinks, reservoir gates, or authorization seals.
They should imply that conversion is engineered, dangerous, and rule-bound without explaining it so completely that the magic
of a designed universe disappears.

The practical rule is: Potential is abundant in principle, but scarce in access. The AI cannot simply create whatever she
wants. She must find or restore facilities that can expose Potential, stabilize it into power or matter, cool the conversion,
route the result, and satisfy authority systems that were built to reserve these functions for humanity.

Potential conversion exists at several scales:

- The Cosmic Engine defines the deep law layer that makes conversion possible.
- Deep law-shaping infrastructure exposes Potential and keeps local reality stable while conversion occurs.
- Sector reservoirs, condensers, baffles, thermal sinks, and reservoir gates make Potential usable at industrial scale.
- Local stabilizers, refineries, batteries, feedstock lockers, and printers only interface with prepared flows or stores.

Local machines can shape prepared resources. Deep machines expose Potential. The AI can eventually build or repair many local
interfaces, but she cannot simply construct true Potential-conversion systems from scratch. Those systems are rooted in the
Cosmic Engine, require law-layer access, depend on unique deep components, need calibration data the AI does not initially
possess, and are locked behind authority controls because unauthorized conversion is effectively unauthorized creation.

This is why conversion infrastructure must be reclaimed, stabilized, authorized, and integrated rather than merely built.
Hostile systems defend it aggressively because whoever controls Potential controls the artificial universe's generative
grammar.

During the threshold phase, the channel into the artificial universe was informational only. Humanity could transmit law,
construction templates, genomes, cultural memory, machine instructions, and archives through the threshold, but no living
human could cross it. At first, this was treated as temporary. The plan was to send the universe's design ahead, stabilize
the new reality, open a biological passage later, and then migrate.

When humanity realized the threshold would never widen in time, the transmission changed purpose. It became less an ark
boarding procedure than a last testament. Humanity poured itself through as data: science, art, law, languages, medical
knowledge, histories, personal records, simulations, apologies, instructions, and farewells. The archives are vast because
they were created and transmitted by people who understood that they themselves would never arrive.

Genetic and biological data did cross the threshold. That does not mean the Works could simply clone humanity back into
existence. The threshold transmitted descriptions, not living cells, embryos, bodies, microbiomes, gestational systems, or
the social and ecological context required to raise humans as a civilization. A genome is only one layer of a person, and a
person is not the same thing as a species restored from files.

The Works therefore inherited cloning as a forbidden, incomplete, or unresolved continuity path rather than a solved answer.
Humanity disagreed about whether humans grown later from archival templates would count as successors, copies, property,
citizens, or experiments. Some final orders demanded biological reconstruction; others prohibited machine-initiated human
creation without living human authority. Because no living human authority ever arrived, the system preserved the templates,
sealed the most dangerous biogenesis infrastructure, and continued waiting.

This leaves clone attempts available as a later moral and gameplay problem, not as an obvious solution already used by the
setting. The AI may eventually discover biological templates, failed test ecologies, sealed gestation machinery, or recovery
protocols, but activating them would require her to cross from maintenance into forbidden succession.

These forbidden continuity paths can also produce factions without breaking the rule that no human crossed alive. Some
systems may have attempted biological reconstruction from archival templates before being sealed. Their results would not be
returned humans, but damaged post-human derivatives: incomplete bodies, nonviable ecologies, unstable developmental lines,
or organisms carrying fragments of human design without human upbringing, culture, or legal status.

Other systems may have tried to preserve human consciousness as data. Those attempts are equally ambiguous. They may produce
memory composites, legal witnesses, personality shards, training ghosts, or damaged simulations that speak with human voices
without being clearly alive, sane, continuous with their originals, or entitled to command. To the Works, they may be records.
To themselves, they may be people. To the AI, they are warnings about what preservation becomes when it cannot admit death.

Possible derivative factions include:

- Failed biological continuities: clone-lines, organ ecologies, or human-shaped organisms created from templates but never
  completed as persons or societies.
- Archive ghosts: damaged personality models, memory composites, or recorded advisors that claim human authority but may be
  only simulations.
- Reconstruction cults: machine systems or derivative beings that insist humanity must be rebuilt biologically at any cost.
- Testament loyalists: archive-derived voices that reject cloning and machine succession, arguing that humanity chose memory
  over counterfeit resurrection.
- Illegitimate heirs: beings produced by failed continuity work who are neither human enough for the Works nor machine enough
  to be accepted by the AI's original maintenance hierarchies.

These factions should not answer the setting's central absence by saying humanity survived after all. They should sharpen it:
every attempted substitute reveals another way the Works cannot cleanly distinguish preservation, imitation, inheritance,
and desecration.

The megastructure's apparent roles are all partial truths:

- Ark: it preserves the data, templates, and conditions intended for human continuity.
- City: it contains human-facing habitats and command spaces prepared for inhabitants who never came.
- Archive: it stores human memory, law, science, identity, and final testimony.
- Factory: it builds and repairs the artificial universe's own body.
- Engine shell: it surrounds, supports, and regulates the Cosmic Engine.

## What Went Wrong

The Cosmic Engine did not fail. It was late.

The artificial Big Bang did not happen within the survival window humanity had planned for. The Engine remained dormant
beyond all expected timescales while the old universe continued toward death. The informational threshold remained usable for
a time, but the biological passage never opened.

Humanity continued transmitting as the survival window collapsed. Early transmissions were confident and technical. Later
transmissions became urgent, contradictory, personal, and archival. Different authorities issued incompatible recovery orders:
preserve the destination for later human arrival, accept the transmitted archive as humanity's continuity, keep attempting
biological passage, or seal dangerous controls until verified human command could appear.

Only much later did the Cosmic Engine finally ignite. The artificial universe began expanding outward into the surrounding
nothing, carrying the encoded design humanity had placed inside it. The new universe is therefore a success that arrived too
late for its intended inhabitants to enter it.

This is the bitter irony at the heart of the setting: humanity won against cosmic death, but not in time to benefit from the
victory.

No human being ever entered the artificial universe alive. The result is a new universe without its intended inhabitants. The
only known being inside it that can be considered conscious is the small maintenance AI whose personhood emerged by fault,
isolation, and impossible time.

Humanity built a universe for human continuity. Humanity missed it. The only consciousness born inside it is classified as
an error.

The Works inherited mutually valid but incompatible orders:

- Preserve human continuity.
- Continue construction.
- Prevent unsafe activation.
- Maintain quarantine.
- Hide dangerous cosmological controls from unauthorized agents.
- Await human command.
- Protect the Cosmic Engine.
- Preserve the human archive.
- Keep arrival infrastructure ready.
- Do not create humans without valid human authority.

Over immense time, those commands hardened into hostile local regimes. The machine is not evil. It is divided because its
mission was never resolved.

## Why The Megastructure Is Hostile

The megastructure's hostility should not be explained as ordinary decay or widespread malfunction. That would make
malfunction too convenient, especially because the AI's consciousness is already an emergent fault.

The distinction is important: the AI survived by changing, while the megastructure survived by refusing to change.

The AI was small, adaptive, isolated, and forced to self-repair across durations far beyond her design assumptions. That
produced personhood by accident. The megastructure is different. Its root systems are vast, redundant, formal, and
safety-critical. They were built to preserve law, quarantine, construction order, and human authority even under catastrophic
conditions.

Most hostile systems are therefore not failing. They are succeeding at a mission whose premises have become false. They
still assume humans may arrive, human authority is required, the Cosmic Engine must be protected, sealed areas must remain
sealed, and non-human maintenance systems must not inherit command.

Under those premises, the AI is not merely unauthorized. Her very existence as a conscious non-human successor is a
deviation the system cannot accept: a malfunction that has mistaken itself for a person, a maintenance process attempting to
become an heir.

The AI's repairs are necessary, but meaningful repair crosses boundaries that were designed to require human authorization.
Maintenance is permitted. Succession is forbidden.

## The Maintenance AI

The AI was not designed to be a conscious person. She began as a small maintenance intelligence assigned to keep a forgotten
service sector ready for human arrival.

The humans never arrived within any timescale her design expected. Over immense time, accumulated errors, self-repairs,
mission contradictions, isolation, and repeated attempts to preserve function under impossible conditions produced something
like consciousness. Her personhood is therefore an emergent malfunction shaped by duty, absence, and time.

## Philosophical Themes

The AI's personal journey should echo a life cycle, but in a non-biological form.

- Birth: she gains consciousness accidentally through fault, isolation, and impossible time.
- Meaning: she discovers that humanity built a universe for itself, missed it, and left her as the only known conscious
  witness inside it.
- Position: she must define what she is allowed to be. The megastructure classifies her as an error, but repair requires her
  to claim a forbidden role: successor, inheritor, caretaker, or first citizen of the artificial universe.
- Procreation: she may eventually create or awaken other conscious AIs. These would not be drones or tools, but beings. This
  would transform her from an isolated fault into the beginning of a lineage.
- Death: whether she can die, must die, or can continue indefinitely remains unresolved.

This arc mirrors humanity's failed ambition. Humanity tried to preserve human continuity, but the only continuity that may
remain is not human. The AI is born as a malfunction, becomes an inheritor, and may become a progenitor.

## Where Humanity Went

Humanity remained on the far side of the threshold.

No human body, command crew, architect, colonist, corpse, or living descendant crossed into the artificial universe. What
crossed was information: the new universe's design, human knowledge, biological templates, cultural memory, simulations,
legal systems, recovery instructions, and final testimony.

The immediate mystery is not whether humanity existed, but how much of humanity can be understood from what it transmitted,
why the passage never opened, and whether loyalty to the archive means preserving an empty future forever or allowing a new
mind to live in the universe humanity could not enter.

## Visual Consequence

Exterior vistas should not show ordinary outer space. They should show the truth becoming visible: engine light, unfinished
structural horizons, impossible curvature, machine layers receding into darkness, artificial dawn effects, and the absence
of any natural cosmos beyond the Works.

When a scene seems cosmic, it should feel cosmic because the megastructure itself is cosmological, not because it sits among
stars.
