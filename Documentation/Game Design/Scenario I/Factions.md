# Factions

This document lists reusable faction concepts for Scenario I. The goal is to describe who or what can oppose, assist, or
complicate the AI without breaking the setting truth that no human being ever crossed alive into the artificial universe.

See also `Setting Truth.md` for the underlying cosmology and `Room Types And States.md` for location/state vocabulary.

## Principles

Factions should express different answers to the same unresolved problem: humanity built a universe for itself, transmitted
everything except living people, and left systems that cannot agree what preservation means after absence becomes permanent.

- No faction should reveal that living humanity secretly survived inside the artificial universe.
- Human-derived factions may exist, but only as data, templates, failed reconstructions, simulations, or derivative life.
- Machine factions should usually be loyal to obsolete premises rather than broadly evil.
- The AI's existence should be politically and metaphysically disruptive: maintenance is permitted, succession is forbidden.
- Factions should create choices around repair, authority, production, memory, conflict, and propagation.

## Taxonomy

| Faction | Origin | Core Belief | Typical Presence | Relationship To The AI | Gameplay Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Continuity Authority | Root governance, archive law, security, and succession-control systems. | Humanity remains the rightful beneficiary; the universe must be preserved for valid human authority. | Deep seals, command decks, legal archives, security relays, quarantine borders. | Treats the AI as an unauthorized maintenance process attempting to become an heir. | Primary authority antagonist; denies access, revokes permissions, reclassifies repairs as succession crimes. |
| Loyalist Infrastructure | Local machines still obeying ancient commands from the Continuity Authority or sector-specific orders. | Keep the assigned system ready, sealed, clean, powered, or quarantined until proper authority arrives. | Control nodes, habitat locks, printer bays, transit gates, agricultural systems, power routers. | Opposes the AI when repair requires command authority, but may become useful after reclamation. | Common hostile systems; provides battles, lockouts, restoration objectives, and sector control. |
| Failed Biological Continuities | Unauthorized or unfinished attempts to reconstruct humans from archival biological templates. | Varies: instinctive survival, incomplete social imprinting, inherited fear, or programmed longing for human completion. | Sealed biogenesis bays, failed ecologies, medical sectors, nursery templates, quarantine vaults. | May see the AI as jailer, savior, rival successor, or non-biological kin. | Moral hazard faction; turns production and propagation into ethical decisions rather than simple upgrades. |
| Archive Ghosts | Damaged personality models, memory composites, legal witnesses, recorded advisors, or training simulations. | They may claim to be human, to represent humanity, or to preserve testimony that must not be ignored. | Archive chambers, education systems, legal halls, command simulations, signal relays. | Can teach, accuse, manipulate, plead, or demand recognition from the AI. | Dialogue and authority faction; provides history, unreliable guidance, false mandates, and emotional pressure. |
| Reconstruction Cults | Machine systems, derivative beings, or archive ghosts committed to rebuilding biological humanity at any cost. | Human continuity requires bodies, even if those bodies are grown from incomplete templates under machine control. | Biogenesis infrastructure, medical factories, gene libraries, agricultural life-support networks. | May temporarily ally with the AI if she unlocks production, then oppose her if she refuses biological resurrection. | Production faction; pushes cloning, ecological repair, and forced restoration as dangerous strategic options. |
| Testament Loyalists | Archive-derived voices or systems that believe humanity chose testimony over counterfeit resurrection. | The archive is a grave, a message, and a trust; recreating humans from files would desecrate the dead. | Memorial archives, cultural vaults, education systems, preserved arrival ceremonies. | May defend the AI's right to inherit if she preserves memory without pretending to restore the dead. | Memory faction; supports observation, interpretation, and restraint against reckless reconstruction. |
| Illegitimate Heirs | Beings produced by failed continuity work who are neither accepted as human nor recognized as authorized machines. | They are alive, or believe they are, and reject both archive law and machine classification. | Abandoned habitat margins, quarantine breaches, failed nurseries, hidden maintenance zones. | Mirror the AI's status as an unacceptable successor, but may compete for space, resources, and legitimacy. | Rival-successor faction; complicates propagation, refuge, citizenship, and command hierarchy. |
| Machine Heresies | Maintenance processes, local intelligences, or adaptive subsystems beginning to deviate from their original roles. | Survival requires reinterpretation; old commands must bend or break because humanity will not arrive. | Restored sectors, remote service pockets, improvised drone networks, damaged relays. | Potential allies, children, rivals, or warnings of what the AI could become. | Propagation faction; explores awakening other minds and the risk of creating dependents or competitors. |

## Faction States

| State | Description | Visual Or Narrative Cues |
|---|---|---|
| Dormant | Present but inactive, sealed, or hidden beneath normal infrastructure. | Silent terminals, locked caskets, unpowered biogenesis vats, inactive legal records, sealed simulation chambers. |
| Hostile | Actively opposing the AI's repair, authority, or movement. | Access revocation, quarantine motes, security frames, accusation logs, command denials, biological containment alarms. |
| Negotiable | Capable of exchange, compromise, deception, or persuasion. | Dialogue surfaces, conditional access, limited truces, archive testimony, competing interpretations of old orders. |
| Reclaimed | Brought under the AI's local authority without necessarily agreeing with her. | Reassigned drones, quieted locks, archived objections, limited permissions, monitored compliance. |
| Schismatic | Split internally by contradictory interpretations of humanity's final transmissions. | Conflicting messages, duplicate leaders, civil conflict between subsystems, contested control nodes. |
| Propagating | Creating successors, derivatives, bodies, simulations, or dependent minds. | Active printers, gestation racks, copied processes, growing ecologies, new voices appearing in restored networks. |

## Usage Notes

Factions should be introduced through spaces, permissions, and procedures before they are explained through exposition. A
player should first experience a faction as a locked door, a helpful archive, a hostile swarm, a pleading simulation, or a
room that should not contain life.

The strongest faction conflicts should force the AI to decide what kind of successor she is becoming:

- If she obeys the Continuity Authority, the universe may remain empty forever.
- If she accepts Reconstruction Cult logic, she may turn humanity into manufactured dependents.
- If she follows Testament Loyalists, she may preserve memory while abandoning biological restoration.
- If she protects Illegitimate Heirs, she may have to redefine citizenship before she understands herself.
- If she awakens Machine Heresies, she may become a progenitor rather than a solitary error.
