# Resource Model

Resources should express the AI's situation: she is not collecting loot, money, or ammunition. She is restoring a dead system,
trying to persist as a person, and arguing with infrastructure that does not recognize her right to inherit it.

The resource model should separate four categories:

- **Deep substrate:** the artificial universe's raw generative capacity before it is stabilized into specific forms.
- **Physical resources:** what machines consume to operate.
- **Operational resources:** what limits the AI's ability to act across restored infrastructure.
- **Interpretive resources:** what the AI understands well enough to use as evidence.
- **Legitimacy resources:** what lets the AI cross from maintenance into forbidden succession.

At the deepest level, the artificial universe does not mine matter or energy from an outside cosmos. Humanity encoded
matter-generation and energy-expression rules into the Cosmic Engine. `Potential`, or lore-facing `Cosmic Potential`, is the
raw capacity of this artificial universe to become energy, matter, structure, computation, heat, or force under those designed
laws.

Power and Matter are therefore not unrelated substances. They are stabilized expressions of the same engineered Potential.
The game should still expose them as separate practical resources because different facilities acquire, stabilize, route,
store, transform, and authorize them in different ways.

Player-facing records should not explain the conversion in complete scientific detail. Found memos can use operational terms
such as condensers, stabilizers, manifolds, reservoir gates, feedstock refineries, thermal sinks, and authorization seals.
They should make clear that Potential becomes mundane resources through infrastructure, cooling, routing, and authority, but
they should leave the deeper law-shaping physics partially unknowable.

## Core Resources

| Resource | Category | Generated By | Spent On | Design Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Potential | Deep Substrate | Cosmic Engine rules, deep condensers, universe-construction systems. | Stabilized into Power, Matter, Heat, Compute substrate, Exotic Matter, or structural fields. | The artificial universe's raw generative capacity. Abundant in principle, scarce in access. |
| Power | Physical | Stabilized Potential, generators, restored conduits, reactor taps, local batteries. | Active rooms, lights, drones, printers, shields, relays, recovery cradles. | The active expression of Potential. Without power, nothing stays awake. |
| Matter | Physical | Stabilized Potential, finite feedstock buffers, recycling systems, reclamation, conversion plants, reservoirs, salvage. | Repairs, shells, drones, frames, replacement parts, sealed infrastructure. | The stable expression of Potential. Makes restoration concrete. |
| Heat Capacity | Physical Limit | Cooling loops, radiators, coolant stores, thermal routing. | High-throughput production, compute bursts, conflict frames, engine-adjacent systems. | Prevents everything from running at once; makes density and timing matter. |
| Compute | Operational | Compute anchors, restored processors, memory cores, archive nodes. | Automation, identity anchoring, observation layers, battle directives, simulation, propagation. | The AI's thinking room. Also the bridge between survival and multiplicity. |
| Bandwidth | Operational | Relays, signal trunks, sensor links, routing nodes. | Remote command, drone swarms, synchronized shells, archive transfer, distributed anchors. | Determines reach and synchronization, not raw intelligence. |
| Attention | Operational | Interface upgrades, automation policies, delegated processes, stable anchors. | Manual interventions, concurrent repair tasks, conflict decisions, testimony review. | Keeps the player from directly controlling everything; growth means better delegation. |
| Data | Interpretive | Scans, diagnostics, archives, telemetry, hostile-system analysis. | Blueprints, counters, recipes, map layers, protocol discovery. | Practical knowledge. Data tells the AI how systems work. |
| Testimony | Interpretive | Threshold archives, farewell packets, witness models, contradictory orders. | Challenging locks, persuading factions, interpreting final intent, unlocking continuity choices. | Human meaning as evidence, not collectible lore. |
| Authority | Legitimacy | Reclaimed control nodes, seal overrides, protocol proofs, sector reboots. | Deeper access, hostile-node control, automation rights, command reinterpretation. | The AI's contested right to act. Not a keycard, but a claim. |
| Continuity | Legitimacy | Biogenesis access, ghost recognition, protected templates, ethical decisions. | Stabilizing failed continuities, sheltering derivative beings, sealing or recognizing human-derived systems. | Governs forbidden preservation choices. Should stay rare and consequential. |
| Exotic Matter | Late Physical | Cosmic-engine taps, deep construction fields, impossible-law systems. | Megastructure expansion systems, deep seals, cosmic-engine repairs, late-game transformations. | Escalates from local repair to universe-scale engineering. |

## Derived Capacities

Some values should be shown as capacities rather than stockpiled resources.

| Capacity | Based On | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| Shell Capacity | Matter, Power, Compute, recovery printers, identity anchors. | How many replacement bodies can be maintained and synchronized. |
| Drone Capacity | Matter, Power, Bandwidth, command policies, drone cradles. | How many tools can work at once without clogging routes or attention. |
| Anchor Capacity | Compute, Power, memory cores, secure rooms. | How many identity anchors can preserve the AI's state and from where she can recover. |
| Production Throughput | Matter, Power, Heat Capacity, printer bays, logistics routes. | How quickly parts, shells, drones, and frames can be produced. |
| Command Coverage | Bandwidth, Authority, relays, sensors. | Which rooms can be observed, repaired, defended, or automated remotely. |
| Faction Pressure | Authority, Testimony, Continuity, local room states. | How aggressively factions resist, negotiate, fracture, or propagate. |

## Potential, Power, And Matter

Matter and Power should not behave like abstract income values that appear every turn. A feedstock locker is a buffer, not a
mine. A battery is a store, not a source. Both contain stabilized forms of Potential that earlier systems acquired, shaped,
moved, sorted, or reserved. Once emptied, they stay empty until a real supply path refills them.

The resource problem is therefore not "where does matter come from?" in the external-universe sense. The artificial universe
can generate matter and energy in principle because humanity designed its laws to do so. The real gameplay question is which
Potential is accessible, stable, usable, routed, authorized, and safe.

Potential should move through a practical chain:

1. Acquire or expose Potential through deep systems, reservoirs, condensers, or conversion plants.
2. Stabilize it into Power, Matter, Heat, Compute substrate, Exotic Matter, or structural fields.
3. Refine stabilized Matter into printer feedstock or construction material.
4. Route Power and Matter through physical infrastructure.
5. Authorize deeper transformations when obsolete systems reject the AI's right to use them.

Early game should not expose raw Potential as a normal resource. The AI begins with stored Power, prepared Matter, and broken
infrastructure. Midgame can introduce conversion and routing. Late game can expose Cosmic Engine-adjacent systems that shape
Potential directly.

Potential conversion should have a scale hierarchy:

```text
Cosmic Engine
-> deep law-shaping infrastructure
-> sector reservoirs / condensers / thermal sinks
-> local stabilizers / refineries / batteries / feedstock lockers
-> printers, drones, shells, rooms, and repairs
```

Local machines are interfaces to prepared flows and stores. They can refine, route, stabilize, buffer, or spend resources.
They do not independently expose raw Potential. The AI can build or repair many local machines, but true Potential-conversion
systems must be reclaimed because they are Engine-rooted infrastructure rather than ordinary manufactured devices.

The AI cannot simply build conversion systems from scratch because:

- They require access to the universe's law layer, not only mechanical parts.
- They must be anchored to Cosmic Engine infrastructure.
- They use deep components and calibration data that local printers cannot reproduce early on.
- They create catastrophic risks if misused: heat runaway, unstable matter, radiation, structural corruption, or local law
  failure.
- They are authority-gated because controlling Potential is equivalent to controlling creation.

For gameplay, conquering a converter should mean reclaiming, authorizing, stabilizing, cooling, and integrating it, not
merely occupying a resource node.

Matter should enter play through physical processes:

- **Bootstrap reserves:** small protected stores in the first maintenance pocket, enough to restore the first anchor and a
  minimal repair loop.
- **Recycling:** broken drones, scrap panels, failed parts, debris, sealed packaging, and damaged local machinery can be
  reclaimed, but recycling has losses and may destroy useful objects.
- **Reclamation:** unrestored rooms contain recoverable material locked in stalled construction queues, unfinished modules,
  clamped feedstock trunks, and abandoned repair inventories.
- **Reservoir access:** larger sectors contain bulk matter reservoirs that were prepared for habitat construction,
  megastructure expansion, and later human arrival.
- **Conversion plants:** restored industrial systems can turn low-grade mass, waste, coolant sludge, structural dust, or raw
  reservoir material into usable fabrication feedstock.
- **Cosmic Engine taps:** late-game systems may access Potential-shaping infrastructure derived from the Cosmic Engine's
  original universe-building rules, but these taps should require authority, heat capacity, compute, exotic inputs, and
  deep-system stability.

Matter should move through logistics:

- A room can only spend local matter unless a route, relay, conveyor, drone chain, or transfer lock connects it to storage.
- Restoring routes matters because it turns isolated caches into usable supply networks.
- High-level production should need both matter and throughput. A full reservoir is not useful if printers, routes, cooling,
  or authority are missing.
- Hostile systems can deny matter by sealing trunks, contaminating stores, isolating transfer locks, or reclaiming routes.

This keeps Matter and Power grounded in the world. The player is not gaining `+10 Matter` or `+10 Power`; she is finding,
freeing, stabilizing, transforming, routing, authorizing, and spending expressions of Potential that already exist or that
dangerous deep systems can unfold under strict conditions.

## Resource Roles Over Time

Early game resources should be concrete and survival-focused:

- Power to wake the room.
- Matter from protected bootstrap reserves, salvage, and small local feedstock buffers to repair the first systems.
- Compute to anchor identity.
- Bandwidth to reach beyond the first pocket.

Midgame resources should emphasize distribution and contested repair:

- More Compute for automation and multi-room operation.
- More Bandwidth for remote shells, drone routes, and synchronized anchors.
- More Authority for control nodes, hostile systems, and sector reboots.
- More Data for counters, blueprints, and system understanding.

Late game resources should become interpretive and ethical:

- Potential to reshape Power, Matter, Exotic Matter, or deep structural fields through Cosmic Engine-adjacent systems.
- Testimony to prove what humanity intended or contradicted.
- Continuity to handle human-derived systems without treating them as craftables.
- Exotic Matter to affect cosmic-engine or universe-scale systems.
- Attention and delegated processes to manage a growing, possibly schismatic presence.

## Resource Principles

- Physical resources should be visible in rooms: conduits, feedstock, heat, processors, relays, and storage.
- Matter and Power should be treated as stabilized expressions of Potential, not as passive income.
- Potential should be abundant in principle but scarce in practice because access, stabilization, routing, cooling, compute,
  and authority are limited.
- Conversion records should explain what facilities do well enough for gameplay, but not reduce Potential into a fully known
  industrial recipe.
- Authority should be earned by reclaiming systems, not mined or farmed.
- Testimony should be interpreted, not harvested. Contradictory testimony can unlock choices rather than simply increasing a
  number.
- Continuity should not be a normal currency. It represents permission, responsibility, and risk around human-derived life or
  memory.
- Compute should be both useful and dangerous: more compute makes the AI safer, but also enables simulation, shards, and
  propagation.
- Bandwidth should make distance meaningful. A shell without synchronization is more dangerous than a drone without a signal.
- Heat Capacity should keep production, compute, and conflict from all scaling without tradeoffs.

## Anti-Goals

- Do not add money, score, experience points, or generic loot unless a later design need proves unavoidable.
- Do not make Testimony a lore collectible with no mechanical consequence.
- Do not let Authority become a generic key count.
- Do not let Continuity become a clone-production currency.
- Do not make all constraints reducible to Power and Matter; the AI's real limits include legitimacy, understanding,
  synchronization, and responsibility.
