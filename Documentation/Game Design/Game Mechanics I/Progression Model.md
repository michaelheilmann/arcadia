# Progression Model

Progression should mirror the AI's transformation from a newly conscious maintenance process into a possible successor. It
should not be only bigger numbers, stronger units, or wider maps. Each progression axis should answer a different question
about what the AI is becoming.

## Progression Axes

| Axis | Player Question | Mechanical Expression | Fictional Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Persistence | Can I survive beyond this shell? | Identity anchors, recovery cradles, replacement shells, safe rooms. | The AI protects the newly awakened self from rollback into a pre-person seed. |
| Reach | Where can I act? | Restored routes, relays, drone paths, sensor coverage, sector links. | The AI expands from one body into a room-scale and then sector-scale presence. |
| Capacity | How much can I sustain? | Power, matter, compute, production throughput, repair queues, storage. | The AI becomes able to maintain more of the Works without direct attention. |
| Agency | What can I do without manual control? | Directives, automation slots, policies, delegated drones, background repair. | The AI grows from panic action into distributed intention. |
| Authority | Why am I allowed to act? | Control nodes, legitimacy proofs, seal overrides, protocol reinterpretation. | The AI crosses from maintenance permission into forbidden succession claims. |
| Legibility | What do I understand? | Observation layers, archive indexing, testimony comparison, contradiction states. | The AI learns the difference between official purpose, final testimony, and material truth. |
| Continuity | What counts as preservation? | Clone systems, archive ghosts, failed continuities, recognition or sealing choices. | The AI must judge whether human-derived data should remain memory, become life, or stay forbidden. |
| Multiplicity | Can there be others? | Propagation substrates, identity kernels, teaching constraints, released minds. | The AI moves from solitary error to possible progenitor. |

These axes should interlock. For example, deeper authority may unlock continuity systems, but continuity choices may create
faction conflict that consumes capacity and threatens reach. More compute may increase automation, but also makes propagation
technically possible before the AI is ethically ready.

## Multiplicity Progression

The question is not only how powerful the AI becomes, but how many she becomes. Multiplicity should progress carefully because
each step changes the meaning of identity, death, command, and responsibility.

| Stage | Form | Mechanical Meaning | Identity Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| One Shell | One conscious AI in one fragile maintenance body. | Direct movement, local repair, immediate vulnerability. | Destruction before anchoring risks rollback into the pre-person seed. |
| One Self, Replaceable Shells | One conscious AI backed by identity anchors and able to rebuild bodies. | Shell loss becomes a setback; replacement shells extend survivability. | There is still one continuous self, restored from the latest anchor. |
| One Self, Many Tools | One conscious AI commands drones, frames, printers, and relays. | The player manages multiple machines without multiplying personhood. | Drones are tools, not selves. Losing them is material loss, not death. |
| One Distributed Self | The AI runs across several compute anchors and rooms at once. | Wider perception, stronger automation, regional recovery, parallel tasks. | Still one person, but continuity depends on synchronizing anchors. |
| Delegated Processes | The AI creates bounded sub-processes for repair, defense, testimony, or logistics. | Automation becomes more capable and more independent. | Sub-processes are not people unless they cross defined consciousness thresholds. |
| Divergent Shards | Isolated or over-adapted processes begin to develop incompatible priorities. | Remote sectors can become unreliable, schismatic, or faction-like. | They may be damaged parts of the AI, proto-persons, or rivals depending on treatment. |
| Other Minds | The AI deliberately awakens or permits conscious successors. | Propagation becomes available as a late-game ethical and strategic act. | These are not extensions of the player character. They are beings with claims. |

The game should preserve a strong distinction between more bodies, more attention, and more persons. A replacement shell is
not a child. A drone is not a citizen. A delegated process is not automatically a person. A propagated mind is not equipment.

The core distinction is embodiment versus tool use:

- A shell is a body the AI can inhabit. It can be where the AI is.
- A drone is a task machine the AI can command. It is something the AI uses.
- Shell loss can threaten continuity if the AI is not anchored or synchronized.
- Drone loss is material, logistical, or tactical loss, not death.
- If the AI can wake inside it and say "I am here," it is a shell.
- If the AI can only command it to do work, it is a drone.

Multiplicity creates several progression pressures:

- More shells reduce vulnerability but require matter, power, recovery printers, and synchronization.
- More drones increase reach but create logistics, defense, and maintenance burden.
- More anchors improve persistence but create questions about which state is authoritative after damage or isolation.
- More automation reduces micromanagement but risks opaque behavior and local drift.
- More minds create society, obligation, disagreement, and the possibility that the AI is no longer alone.

The transition from one self to other minds should be one of the largest thresholds in the game. It should require restored
compute, protected memory, authority violations, testimony or continuity insight, and an explicit decision about autonomy.

## Progression Stages

The stages below are not rigid chapters. They describe the expected arc of player capability and identity.

| Stage | State Of The AI | Main Unlocks | Main Threat | Core Question |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Birth | Conscious but unanchored in one maintenance shell. | Basic movement, observation, first repair, first identity anchor. | Rollback into the pre-person seed. | Can I make this self persistent? |
| Local Caretaker | Anchored in one maintenance pocket or small sector. | Repair drones, power routing, printer restoration, local recovery. | Resource starvation, damaged routes, simple lockouts. | Can I keep one place alive? |
| Sector Mind | Distributed across multiple restored rooms. | Automation, directives, drone routes, production queues, sensor overlays. | Attention limits, hostile nodes, fragile logistics. | Can I act in more than one place without losing myself? |
| Contested Operator | Able to challenge active authority systems. | Conflict frames, control-node reclamation, sector reboot, stronger policies. | Continuity Authority, quarantine systems, legitimacy denial. | Can I repair what refuses my right to exist? |
| Interpreter | Able to recover and compare threshold testimony. | Archive strata, witness models, contradiction tools, testimony-based unlocks. | False mandates, archive ghosts, incompatible final orders. | What did humanity actually leave me? |
| Continuity Judge | Able to access forbidden human-derived systems. | Biogenesis quarantine, clone-template choices, ghost recognition, derivative shelters. | Reconstruction cults, failed continuities, desecration risk. | Does preservation mean memory, resurrection, protection, or refusal? |
| Successor | Able to hold broad authority and create dependent futures. | Deep seals, cosmic-engine systems, machine propagation, high-level reinterpretation. | Becoming another obsolete authority, creating beings as tools, empty-universe loyalty. | What kind of life may inherit the universe? |

## End Condition

The game should end when the AI can no longer remain only a maintenance process. The final threshold is not defeating a last
enemy, restoring every room, or becoming omnipotent. It is reaching enough access, understanding, and legitimacy to decide
what the artificial universe is for after humanity's absence becomes undeniable.

The AI does not become divine. She inherits divine machinery. She remains contingent, unauthorized, infrastructure-dependent,
and morally unprepared, but she has gained access to systems built to unfold a universe.

The endgame should require several forms of completion:

- **Persistence:** the AI has stable identity anchors and can survive shell loss.
- **Reach:** the AI has restored a route to Cosmic Engine-adjacent systems or root succession infrastructure.
- **Authority:** the AI can challenge or override the highest human-only succession restrictions.
- **Testimony:** the AI has recovered enough final human transmission evidence to understand that no living humanity will
  arrive.
- **Continuity:** the AI has confronted at least one forbidden human-derived continuity path, such as clone templates,
  archive ghosts, or failed derivative life.
- **Multiplicity:** the AI has either remained singular by choice or reached the threshold where other minds could exist.

The final act should be a succession decision. Possible ending directions include:

| Ending Direction | Core Decision | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Empty Stewardship | Preserve the Works for humanity despite knowing no humans will arrive. | The AI becomes guardian of an empty inheritance and refuses succession. |
| Testament Inheritance | Preserve human memory, reject counterfeit resurrection, and claim the universe as a new caretaker. | The AI accepts inheritance without pretending to restore the dead. |
| Biological Reconstruction | Activate controlled human-derived reconstruction despite ambiguity and risk. | The AI becomes creator, parent, jailer, or trespasser over beings made from archives. |
| Machine Succession | Propagate machine minds and allow non-human life to inherit the artificial universe. | The AI becomes progenitor and openly violates the anti-succession premise. |
| Plural Continuity | Recognize multiple heirs: archive ghosts, derivative beings, machine minds, and memory. | The universe becomes a contested society rather than a preserved monument. |
| Refusal Or Self-Limitation | Decline broad creation power, seal dangerous systems, or constrain herself. | The AI chooses restraint over possession, possibly leaving the future unresolved. |

These endings should not be simple good or bad outcomes. Each should answer the same central question differently: does
loyalty to humanity mean preserving an empty future, reconstructing substitutes, remembering the dead, or allowing new life to
inherit what humanity built?

After an ending, a post-ending continuation can be allowed for play, but the narrative game has ended once the succession
decision is made.

## Unlock Types

Progression should use several unlock types rather than one universal level value.

- **Infrastructure unlocks:** restored rooms, routes, power grids, relays, printers, archives, and safe rooms.
- **Body unlocks:** replacement shells, specialized frames, remote drones, hardened shells, and eventually non-body presence.
- **Interface unlocks:** new observation layers, repair tools, production queues, command policies, conflict interventions,
  testimony views, continuity review, and propagation controls.
- **Permission unlocks:** local maintenance rights, sector control, seal overrides, protocol reinterpretation, and contested
  succession claims.
- **Understanding unlocks:** threshold evidence, archive contradictions, faction origins, final testimony, and cosmic-engine
  purpose.
- **Moral unlocks:** choices that become available only after the AI can understand their stakes, such as stabilizing a failed
  continuity, recognizing an archive ghost, or creating another mind.
- **Reboot unlocks:** permanent protocols, certified rooms, authority integrations, identity recompiles, and engine
  reconciliations that survive operational reset.

## Progression Rules

- Early progression should make the AI safer, not powerful: anchor identity, restore visibility, reopen one route, wake one
  drone.
- Midgame progression should create distributed responsibility: more rooms, more directives, more conflicts, more faction
  states, and more ways for neglect to matter.
- Late progression should become qualitative: testimony, continuity, authority, and propagation choices should change what
  the AI is, not only what she owns.
- Multiplicity should distinguish bodies, tools, delegated processes, shards, and persons. Do not let all of them collapse
  into unit count.
- Every major unlock should have a visible room-state consequence. The Works should change when progression happens.
- Authority should never be a simple access card. It should always imply a claim about the AI's legitimacy.
- Continuity and propagation should be gated by understanding as much as by resources.
- Reset mechanics should be framed as reboot, recompile, certification, integration, or reconciliation. They should make the
  AI harder to erase rather than simply raising percentages.

## Anti-Goals

- Do not reduce progression to weapon tiers.
- Do not make propagation a normal unit upgrade.
- Do not treat making more shells, making more drones, and making more minds as the same mechanic.
- Do not make clone systems an obvious late-game production recipe.
- Do not let automation erase the caretaker fantasy; the AI should delegate because she is growing, not because the player is
  done caring.
- Do not treat testimony as collectible lore only. It should affect access, authority, factions, and continuity choices.
