# Reboot Model

Idle and incremental games often use reset mechanics where the player sacrifices temporary progress for permanent gains. In
this setting, that mechanic should be diegetic. The AI does not prestige. She reboots, recompiles, reconciles, and integrates
deeper protocols into herself or the Works.

The core rule is: the world does not rewind. Temporary operational state is shed, systems fall back to safer configurations,
and the AI preserves something deeper.

## Fictional Basis

The AI cannot fully rewrite herself while relying on unstable, half-restored infrastructure. Major changes require controlled
interruption. During a reboot, local systems may shut down, routes may relock, temporary drones may be lost, and provisional
authority may expire. What survives is what has been anchored, inherited, certified, or integrated.

This makes reset part of the AI's life cycle:

- At first, almost nothing survives interruption.
- Then identity survives shell loss.
- Then rooms can be certified as safe.
- Then sectors can retain baseline authority.
- Then testimony, values, and continuity decisions can persist.
- Eventually, other minds or successors may survive beyond the AI's direct control.

The emotional question behind rebooting is: what can survive erasure?

## Reset Types

| Reset Type | Sacrifice | Permanent Gain | Fiction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shell Reboot | Current shell state, local transient tasks, unsynced diagnostics. | Better body calibration, safer recovery, clearer local diagnostics. | The AI recalibrates embodiment. |
| Room Certification | Temporary room routing, improvised repairs, unstable queues. | Room starts future cycles with safer baseline power, known hazards, or certified paths. | A room becomes harder to lose. |
| Sector Reboot | Sector activity, temporary production chains, provisional drone deployments. | Permanent sector protocol, automation baseline, authority proof, or resource-routing improvement. | A sector is reinitialized under better law. |
| Identity Recompile | Unanchored experiences, unstable delegated processes, temporary self-modifications. | More attention, stronger anchoring, better synchronization, safer shell recovery. | The AI rewrites her self-model without becoming someone else. |
| Authority Integration | Provisional access, spoofed permissions, contested locks. | Durable legitimacy over a class of systems or seals. | The AI turns temporary permission into inherited protocol. |
| Continuity Review | Some forbidden options become sealed, delayed, or morally committed. | Continuity judgments persist and unlock deeper ethical or faction paths. | The AI decides what kinds of preservation she will or will not perform. |
| Engine Reconciliation | Large-scale instability, sector shutdown, possible faction escalation. | Deeper Potential access, cosmic-engine compatibility, ending-path access. | The AI aligns herself with divine machinery without becoming divine. |

## Preserved State

Resets should distinguish temporary state from persistent inheritance.

Usually sacrificed:

- Temporary repairs.
- Local routing hacks.
- Unsynced shell experience.
- Active drone assignments.
- Uncertified production queues.
- Improvised conflict advantages.
- Spoofed or provisional authority.
- Unstable delegated processes.

Usually preserved or improved:

- Identity anchors.
- Certified rooms.
- Reclaimed control-node proofs.
- Blueprints and practical Data.
- Interpreted Testimony.
- Durable Authority integrations.
- Continuity judgments.
- Permanent boot routines and automation policies.
- Safer shell recovery and synchronization rules.

## Design Rules

- Do not call this prestige in player-facing fiction.
- Reboots should feel like controlled interruption, not time travel.
- Permanent gains should be protocols, proofs, certifications, calibrations, or inherited understandings.
- A reset should make the AI harder to erase, not merely increase a percentage.
- Reboot choices should interact with factions. A sector reboot may provoke loyalist systems, silence a ghost, preserve a
  derivative being, or force a schism.
- Reboots should preserve the caretaker fantasy. The player sacrifices temporary control to make the Works more safely alive.

## Stage Use

Early game reboots should be small and protective:

- Rebuild a shell.
- Certify the first maintenance pocket.
- Improve identity anchoring.

Midgame reboots should organize complexity:

- Reinitialize sectors.
- Lock in automation policies.
- Convert temporary control into authority.
- Preserve route and production baselines.

Late game reboots should be qualitative:

- Integrate testimony into law challenges.
- Commit continuity decisions.
- Stabilize or reject divergent shards.
- Reconcile with Cosmic Engine-adjacent systems.

Endgame reboots should feel dangerous because they are no longer only recovery. They are self-definition.
